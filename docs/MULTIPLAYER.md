# Crew mode (play with friends)

Crew mode lets up to four friends fly the very same mission. There are two ways to play it, and the host picks one in the lobby:

- **Each their own base** ("race", the first crew mode). Every friend runs their own game on the same seed and the same real Sun, sees the others' astronauts, and compares crews at the end. Nobody can change anyone else's base. The sections before "One base together" describe it.
- **One base together** ("co-op"). The whole crew shares ONE outpost and ONE mission: they vote on the event card, plan the power together, walk outside together and read one report. See "One base together" below. It is **hidden for now** (see "The kill switch").

It is **opt-in**, it has **no chat and no typed names**, and the single-player game never needs it. Solo play and race mode never run any co-op code: the co-op code starts only when the host started the mission in co-op mode.

## What a child sees (both modes)

- Title screen, **Play with friends**. The lobby page says the rules in plain words (the same words are in `src/i18n/crew.ts`):
  - It is your choice. Crew mode is off until you open it.
  - No chat. No typing names. You can only send small stickers.
  - You get a fun crew name, like "Cadet Comet" ("Кадет Комета").
  - Rooms are private. A friend needs your 6-letter room code.
  - Up to 4 players in a room.
  - Only game numbers travel: week, air, food, radiation, science, your suit colour, and where you walk outside.
  - No account. No ads. No tracking. Leave any time: your own game keeps working.
- **Make a room** gives a code such as `K7M2QX`. Friends type it under **Join**. Everybody presses **Ready**; the host picks world, level and length (and, when the shared base is shown, **One base together** or **Each their own base**) and presses **Start**. The host needs at least one friend, and every seat must speak the same protocol version.
- Everyone flies the same mission: the host's seed and start date go to every friend, so the real NASA space weather, the cards and the sunlight are identical.
- **Friends are in the world.** In a crew mission the two NPC cadets are gone and your friends' astronauts take their place, in each friend's suit colour (the NPCs come back in a solo game).
  - While a friend is on a walk you see them walk (their position is smoothed, and it snaps if they are more than 8 m away). A friend who drives the rover is drawn as the astronaut at the rover's place (no second rover is drawn: the rover is a physics model, and a copy is not cheap). The status message has no "in a building" field, so a friend indoors is noticed by silence: a friend who has just gone out is not drawn until their first position arrives (at most about a second), and if you hear nothing from a walking friend for 3 seconds (a building, or a poor connection) the astronaut goes indoors and the name tag goes. Friends use the same model detail as your own cadet (the light model on low and medium) and are never thinned out by the graphics level.
  - A friend who is not on a walk stands at one of three fixed places by the habitat plaza, with an emote (a drill while planning, a wave at the report). When their game is at night, when they have finished or gone, they are indoors (the NPC cadets also hide in storms; friends do not, so you always see where the crew is).
  - A small name tag floats over each friend (the preset crew name in your language, and a small icon: planning, outside, reading the report) when they are on the screen and within 60 m of what you look at (your walking cadet, or the outpost). A sticker a friend sends also shows as a bubble over their head for 3 seconds, as well as the toast.
  - The corner map shows friends as dots in their suit colour.
- **Weeks go on together.** After the report, **Next week** waits for friends who are still behind (connected, not finished, in an earlier week or at an earlier step of the same week). It says who ("Waiting for the crew: Cadet Comet") and has a **Don't wait** button; it goes on by itself as soon as nobody is behind. A friend who left, finished or lost the connection is never waited for. The last week's **Finish** does not wait (the review shows who is still playing). Solo play is not changed. This is a deliberate change to the "race" mode (each their own base) from before the live avatars: the friends' astronauts, name tags, stickers over heads and this wait all run in it, because the crew is the only multiplayer mode today. The wait is the one place where race mode can hold a player back, so it always has **Don't wait**. The shared base does not use this wait: it has its own Ready check (below). If a shared base ends by itself on a device (the host left, or the child chose to play on alone), that device goes on in race mode and this wait works again.
- In the game a small **Friends** button opens a dock with each friend's week, air, food, radiation and science, and eight stickers
  (Great, Hooray!, Shield up!, Plants!, Need power, Wow!, Thanks!, Oops). A sticker arrives as a small toast. Stickers are limited to one per second.
- On the debrief a table compares the crews: who kept the crew safest (finished, lowest share of the radiation limit), most science, most stars, and one line per friend about their biggest solar-storm choice
  (took cover / shield on / worked through it / no big storm).
- Offline or blocked network: the lobby says so ("No connection... Your own game still works"). Leaving a room, or going back to the title screen, ends the crew; the single-player flow is not touched.
- One thing crew mode does change: a crew mission is saved like any mission, so it replaces an earlier saved solo mission (the lobby warns about it when a saved mission exists). Your next solo setup (world, level, length) is put back to what it was.

## One base together (co-op)

Two to four friends share one outpost and one mission. Each device still draws the whole 3D world itself; what is shared is the mission's numbers and the decisions. The host's device is the **commander**: it runs the rules, and every other device is a **mirror** that shows what the commander says. (This is not lockstep: guests do not run the week themselves.)

### What a child sees

One pattern on every waiting screen: a row of dots in the crew's suit colours with a tick for each friend who has answered, and a countdown that starts when the first friend answers. At zero the commander goes on with those who answered.

| Stage | What the crew does | Countdown (from the first answer) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Forecast (briefing) | Everybody presses ready | 20 s |
| Event card | Everybody votes for an option. The most votes win; on a tie the commander's vote wins if it is one of the tied options, then Mission Control's advice, then the first option; with no vote at all Mission Control's advice wins. An option the crew cannot take cannot be voted | 30 s |
| Event result | Everybody presses ready | 20 s |
| Power plan | Everybody sets the same plan, live: a changed row flashes with the friend's name, and any change clears every Ready ("The plan changed. Look again.") | 30 s after the first Ready, but the plan ends 90 s after the first Ready whatever happens |
| Walk | First everybody picks **walk** or **stay inside** (20 s from the first pick). Then the walkers go out and each plays their own walk; the others see "N friends outside". The stage ends when every walker is back | 170 s after the walkers went out (the longest walk the game can give, 110 s, plus 60 s) |
| Night | Nobody waits: the commander works the night out at once and the report comes up | none |
| Report | Everybody presses **Next week** | 20 s |

- The commander has a **Go on** button, shown after 5 seconds on a stage, only while somebody has not answered yet and only after the commander answered too. It goes on now with those who answered.
- Build, print and room games work in any daytime stage (forecast to walk, never at the report or at night). Each is sent to the commander, who decides. Everybody sees the same outpost, buildings and materials; the printer and the five room games are played on the child's own device and only the result goes to the commander.
- In a room, a chip tells what the crew is doing ("The crew is planning power"). If the stage moves on while a child is in a room, a film, a room game, the printer or a walk, the new stage waits until that closes. At night everything is closed for everybody: the film, the room (a room game is not paid), the printer and a walk still running.
- A walk result is not applied one by one. The commander waits for every walker, then merges them into ONE result and applies it once (the game allows one walk a week): science is the union of the samples picked up (every walker sees the same six samples), never more than the best single walker could bring home alone (17); finds are the union; a chore done by anybody counts; seconds are the longest walk; materials are the most any walker brought, per kind; if some walkers were caught the dose penalty is scaled by the share of walkers who were caught. A crew of four therefore cannot out-earn one good solo walker.
- **Friends in the world.** The friends' astronauts, name tags, stickers and map dots work as in race mode. On the shared base the commander's stage says who is outside: a friend is drawn on a walk only while the commander says the walkers are out, this friend chose to walk and is not back yet. Everybody else stands at the base, with the "planning" or "reading the report" icon. At night and at the end everybody is indoors. The two helper cadets are replaced by the friends as in race mode.
- **One result.** The debrief shows one result for the crew (how it ended, stars, science, radiation) and one line per friend with what they did: samples picked, finds, room games won, things printed, votes cast. Each device runs the end of the mission on its own profile: your badges, best stars, rank and suits are yours (see the trust limits for what that means).
- **A friend leaves.** The commander lets a seat go 5 seconds after that player left the room (not while the commander's own connection is down). The crew goes on without them: they are taken out of the ready, vote and walk lists, and a walk result already sent still counts. Their part in the debrief stays.
- **The commander leaves, or goes quiet.** A guest that has heard nothing for 15 seconds shows "Waiting for the commander…". From 45 seconds of silence a **Play on alone** button appears. If the commander has been out of the room for 20 seconds, or the child presses the button, or the commander's state no longer lists this seat, the guest leaves the shared base: the mission stays exactly as it is, a walk that was finished but not yet taken in is applied to the child's own mission, and the game goes on in the child's own base, as in race mode. There is no new commander in this version.
- **Reload.** The player id is new on every visit, so a child who reloads cannot rejoin the crew. Their saved mission (the last state the commander sent, with their own plan) comes back through **Continue**, and Continue always plays alone.
- **Rules that do not change.** Creative mode and the tutorial are off in a crew. Nobody can join after the start. A crew mission is saved like any mission (see above).

### The kill switch

The lobby shows the **One base together / Each their own base** choice when `coopVisible()` in `src/net/config.ts` is true: the constant `COOP_DEFAULT_VISIBLE` (now `true`), the address `?coop=1`, or a build made with `VITE_COOP=1`. It was switched on after a two-browser pass on the live site: make a room, join, choose One base together, start, and both players see the same mission and move from the forecast to the power plan together. A pass on two real devices (phones on mobile data, a dropped connection) is still to come. When the choice is shown, **One base together** is selected first. Setting the constant back to `false` hides the choice again; the co-op code stays in the build and runs only when a host starts a mission in co-op mode.

Nothing the player typed is sent: not their name (the typed cadet name from the Setup screen stays on the device), not their saved progress, not their location. There is no account, no cookie, no analytics. The id changes on every visit, so nobody can be followed from one day to the next.
Every incoming message is checked (`src/net/protocol.ts` for the event name and the size, `src/net/coopwire.ts` and `src/sim/coop.ts` for the shared-base messages): known event, exact shape, number ranges, and a size limit (700 characters; 3000 for `st`, 1200 for `rec`); anything else is dropped. Even a hand-made client cannot put words on another child's screen, because the network carries numbers and ids, and the text always comes from the viewer's own language file.

### Signed messages: who sent it

The channel is public and has no accounts, so the network itself cannot tell who sent a message. The game proves it instead: every message on the channel, in race mode and in the shared base, is signed, and so are the parts of a presence entry that carry decisions (the host's start, `s` and `p`, and the host's seat list).

- **One key pair per visit.** When a player opens the crew screen, their copy makes a fresh key pair (ECDSA P-256 through the browser's own Web Crypto). The private half never leaves the device, cannot be read even by the game's own code, and is gone when the page closes. The public half travels in the player's presence entry.
- **What is signed.** A signature covers the wire version, the room code, the sender's id, a counter (or the state revision) and the message itself, and every visit signs with its own key, so a signature cannot be moved to another room, another sender or visit, an older counter or other content.
- **Pinning.** A guest pins the host's public key when it locks the host in (when it joins). The host pins each player's key when it gives that player a seat, and the host's signed seat list carries the seated keys to everyone. From then on a message counts only if it verifies against the pinned key of the id it claims. A message signed with another key, or not signed, is dropped. A later presence entry that shows a different key for a pinned id never replaces the pin; that id is treated like the "two entries" case below.
- **What this closes.** Once a seat is locked, nobody without that seat's private key can send as that seat: not a stranger who knows the room code, not another seated friend. Shared-base state (`st`, `rec`) and the start come only from the pinned host key. Race numbers and stickers under a friend's id, a forged Ready or vote, a forged commander state and a copied host presence entry are all refused.
- **What stays open (honest residuals).**
  - Pinning is trust on first use. Before a guest has locked in the host, or before the host has seated a player, the first key seen for an id is the one that gets pinned, so someone already in the lobby with the room code at that exact moment could get there first. Before the start, the room code is still the only door.
  - A modified host still decides the shared mission and can hand its guests impossible results (see "Honest limits of the shared base"). A signature proves who sent a message, not that the sender is honest.
  - A device without Web Crypto (an insecure page, such as http on a local network address instead of the https site) cannot sign, so it cannot join a crew; the game says that crew mode needs a secure page and names the https address.
- **No downgrade.** There is no unsigned mode. The wire version went up, a copy of the game with an older version is refused by the version check, and a message without a valid signature is never accepted "for now".

### Honest limits (the trust model)

What else the game does about strangers and modified copies:

- **The room code is the only door.** There is no password. Anyone who learns the code can join an open lobby (31 letters and digits, 6 places, about 887 million codes). Share it only with friends.
- **Only the host can start the mission.** The start is not a message that anyone can send or replay: it is a part of the host's own presence entry (`s` and `p`), and a guest reads it only from the host it locked in when it joined. A stranger who claims to be the host (`h`) is ignored by guests already in the room, and a joiner who finds two hosts in a room refuses to join. Because a presence key is chosen by the client, a stranger could also use the host's id as the key; an id that is used by two different entries is believed for neither, so nobody can speak in the host's name (the price is that such a lobby closes). Not covered: a stranger who copies the host's entry exactly, under the host's key, and waits for the real host's connection to drop for a moment; they could then start a mission for the guests (a real mission from the preset list, never text). This needs a modified client, the room code and exact timing.
- **The host decides who has a seat.** The host lists the seated players (at most four) in its presence; everyone shows exactly that list, so a modified client cannot squeeze in as a fifth player or push a friend out by claiming an early arrival time. A seated stranger cannot be removed by the host in this version (the host can leave and make a new room).
- **Spoofing a seated friend is refused.** Numbers or stickers under a friend's id are dropped unless they verify against that friend's pinned key (see "Signed messages"). Someone who uses a friend's id as their own presence key still runs into the "two entries, believe neither" rule, which can cost that friend a seat in the lobby; once a key is pinned, the other entry is ignored. No text can be forged in any case: only numbers and preset ids exist on the wire.
- **A flaky connection can cost a seat.** The same rule applies if a player's own old connection is still listed with different details after an unclean reconnect (it clears by itself after a short while). In the lobby that player may be told the room is full, and can join again.
- **After the start** a room refuses newcomers (their own copy refuses itself; a raw listener who knows the code could still read the numbers, which are not personal). Only the host's own flag says whether the mission has started; a stranger's flag proves nothing.
- A stranger who sits in the lobby without pressing Ready can keep the host from starting. The host can leave and make a new room with a new code.
- Rate limits, sizes and room rules are enforced by every player's copy of the game. A modified client can spam only what other clients drop.
- The Supabase operator can see connection metadata (IP address, time, room code), as any web host can. The game itself writes no table and keeps nothing on the server: it only uses Realtime broadcast and presence.

### Honest limits of the shared base

Race mode only shows friends' numbers. The shared base is different: **numbers that arrive from the network now become the child's own mission**, and that mission is saved on the device (`fo.mission`, which the save loader does not check again) and feeds the profile at the end (missions flown, badges, best stars per world and level, rank, suits, Science log cards). So this is where checking matters most, and these are the rules and their limits:

- **Only the commander speaks for the mission.** A guest takes `st` and `rec` only from the host id it locked in at the start (the commander's seat id must also be the first seat in the message and the sender), and only while the room was started in co-op mode. A race-mode room never reads these messages. The commander takes `int` and `sync` only from seated players, and an intent speaks for its own seat only (`from` must be the sender). Presence (the lobby) is checked: a stranger who uses a seated friend's id, or the host's id, runs into the "two entries, believe neither" rule above. **Every one of these messages is signed** and checked against the pinned key of the id it claims (see "Signed messages"), so the "`from` must be the sender" rules hold against other players and strangers too, not only for honest copies. The checks above still bound what a message can put in a mission, and a guest that sees only older snapshots in order for two heartbeats follows the commander again, as a second line of defence.
- **Everything is rebuilt, field by field** (`src/net/coopwire.ts`, `src/sim/coop.ts`). Unknown extra fields do not come through. Every id comes from a fixed list (events, options, buildings, tools, rooms and games, finds, Science log cards, effects, flags). Every number is an integer or a finite number inside the range the game can reach (air 0 to 100, stars 0 to 3, the week from 1 to 99, and a state past the mission's own length is refused, and so on), every list has a largest length, and a date must be a real calendar day. There is no text field anywhere: a hand-made client cannot put a word on a child's screen. A message that fails any check is dropped whole.
- **The week records must fit the guest's own plan.** Both devices build the plan from the same seed, so a `rec` whose dates are not the dates of the guest's plan for that week is refused, as is a record with a gap before it (the guest asks for the missing weeks with `sync`).
- **What the checks cannot do.** They check that each value is possible alone, not that the whole is a mission that could be played. A modified commander can hand its guests a state with three stars and a finished mission, buildings the crew never built, or all the Science log cards, and the guests' profiles will count it: missions, badges, best stars and suits earned in a shared base are only as honest as the commander. The same is true for a stranger who takes the commander's place through the lobby weakness described above. The worst that can happen to a guest is a wrong progress record on their own device: a new mission replaces the saved one, but badges and best stars that were already counted stay in the profile. Nothing is stored on a server, no text can be forged, and nothing runs from the network. Do not use shared-base results for anything that needs honest scores.
- **Intents.** A friend's choice is checked by the commander's own rules (a blocked option, no materials, the wrong week or stage, a copy or an older number, a seat not in the crew), so a modified guest can only ask for what the game would allow that seat at that moment: a forged vote, a forged Ready or a walk result inside the numbers a real walk can reach. A walk result is refused whole (not trimmed) when it has more than 120 seconds, 6 samples, 17 science, 4 finds or 12 of a material, and the merge never lets the crew pass one good walker. A copy of an intent is ignored (each seat has its own counter), so the printer's one metal back is not paid twice.
- **Rates.** At most 5 `st` a second are sent, and the receiver takes 12 in 2 seconds from the commander; `rec` 24 in 2 s, `int` 24 in 2 s and `sync` 3 in 2 s from one player. A guest holds at most 16 intents and sends one at a time.

## Protocol

Transport: Supabase Realtime, one channel per room, `fo-crew-<CODE>`, with `broadcast: { self: false }` and presence keyed by the random player id.
The protocol version (`PROTOCOL` in `src/net/protocol.ts`) is 3: the start may carry a mode, the co-op messages exist, and **every message and the host's decisions are signed** (version 2 was the first with co-op messages and no signatures). The host cannot start while a seat reports another version, and a guest does not take a host of another version for a host (it cannot sign: "no room").
Every broadcast payload carries `from` (the sender's id), `q` (the sender's counter) and `sig` (the signature); see "Signatures on the wire" below. The start of the mission is not an event: it travels in the host's presence (see the first row). Events:

| Event | Sent by | Payload | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| presence | everyone | `{ id, n, c, r, h, s, t, v, k, m, mk, p, g }` name index, suit index, ready, host, started, join time, protocol version, this visit's public key `k`; host only: the seat list `m` (at most 4 ids) with the seated players' keys `mk` (same order) and, once started, the mission `p = { choice: { site, difficulty, weeks, windowId }, seed, startDate }`, with `mode: 'coop'` added for one base together (race mode is written without a mode, as before), and its signature `g` over these decisions | the lobby list and the start; `id` must equal the presence key; `startDate` must be the one the window gives; a current-version entry without `k` is not an entry, and neither is a host entry without `g` or with a seat list and key list of different lengths |
| `status` | each player | `{ from, week, step, air, food, dose, doseLimit, science, stars, end, storm: { w, s, k, a } }` | at most 2 per second, latest wins, unchanged status is not resent |
| `final` | each player, once | same as `status` | marks the friend as finished |
| `react` | each player | `{ from, id }` | `id` is one of `great hooray shield plants power wow thanks oops`; at most 1 per second |
| `pos` | each player, on a walk only | `{ from, x, z, a, m }` metres (to 0.01, within 500), heading (within 10), `m` 0, 1 or 2 | at most 5 per second sent (16 per 2 s accepted); drawn by `src/world/friends.ts`; a position that is not numbers in these ranges is dropped |
| `st` | the commander only (co-op) | `{ from, stage, rev, week, seats, ready, votes, walk, outside, returned, left, acks, choice, contrib?, s }`: the co-op meta and, in `s`, the live part of the mission (the plan, the history, the config and creative-mode decor never travel: every device builds its own plan from the seed) | latest wins; at most 5 a second sent, 12 in 2 s accepted; at most 3000 characters (measured in 72 whole-mission tests: a fresh state 0.88 to 0.92 kB, the middle one 1.1 kB, the largest 1.96 kB); sent again every 4 s as a heartbeat; `contrib` (what each friend did) only at the report and the end |
| `rec` | the commander only (co-op) | `{ from, r }`: one finished week | at the night, and again for a guest that asks; at most 24 in 2 s accepted; at most 1200 characters (measured in the same tests: a record is about 0.8 kB, the largest 0.84 kB) |
| `int` | a friend, to the commander (co-op) | `{ from, n, w, s, k, ... }`: its own counter `n`, the week `w` and stage `s` it was meant for, and the kind `k` with its numbers or ids: `ready {on}`, `vote {o}`, `ask`, `alloc {a}`, `safe`, `walk {go}`, `walkEnd {r}`, `find {i}`, `build {id}`, `craft {tool, neat}`, `room {room, game}`, `go` (commander only) | 24 in 2 s accepted; at most 700 characters (measured: a power plan is 99 characters, a walk result with every field at its largest 275); one at a time, repeated after 1.5 s, 3 s, 4.5 s and then every 6 s until the next `st` shows its number in `acks`, or its week or stage has passed |
| `sync` | a friend, to the commander (co-op) | `{ from, have }`: weeks of history the friend has (0 to 99) | at most 1 a second sent, 3 in 2 s accepted; the commander answers with the missing `rec` (at most 6 at once) and a new `st`; also sent at the start, and when the connection comes back |

### Signatures on the wire

The text of "Signed messages: who sent it" above is the promise; this is how it is kept (`src/net/sign.ts`, `src/net/room.ts`).

- **Key.** One ECDSA P-256 key pair per `CrewRoom` (so per visit), made with `crypto.subtle.generateKey(..., extractable = false, ['sign', 'verify'])`. The public key is exported as the 65 raw bytes of the uncompressed point and written in base64url without padding: 87 characters, in `k` of the presence entry. A signature is the 64 raw bytes (r, then s; ECDSA with SHA-256) in base64url: 86 characters. A device without `crypto.subtle` (an http page that is not localhost) cannot make a key: the room fails with the usual "offline" and nothing is sent.
- **What is signed (messages).** The UTF-8 text `fo-crew/<PROTOCOL>/<room code>/<event>/<canonical JSON>`, where the JSON is the whole payload except `sig` (so `from`, `q` and every field of the body). "Canonical" means keys sorted at every level, `undefined` left out and non-finite numbers as `null`, so the order of keys on the wire does not matter. `q` is the sender's counter (1, 2, 3 ... over all its events, never reset in a visit). The signature is added beside the body: `{ from, q, ...body, sig }`, so the messages in the table below keep their fields at the top level. The room takes `q` and `sig` off before the parsers run, so the body checks, the size limits (st 3000, rec 1200, the rest 700, on the body) and the rebuilding field by field are unchanged. A whole payload may be `SIG_OVERHEAD` (120) characters longer than its body limit; a bigger one is dropped before any signature check.
- **What is signed (presence).** The host's entry carries `g`: the signature over `fo-crew/<PROTOCOL>/<room code>/presence/<canonical JSON of { id, h: true, v, k, s, p, m, mk }>`. Only the decisions are covered (the start `s` and `p`, the seat list `m` with the seated keys `mk`); the name, suit, ready flag and join time are not, because they change often and decide nothing. The entry is signed again whenever one of these changes, and it is never published with a signature that does not match what it says. A presence entry comes back from the network with keys in any order and extra fields (`presence_ref`): the room checks the signature over the rebuilt entry, never over the raw object.
- **Pinning.** A room keeps `id -> public key`. The host pins its own key when it opens the room. A guest pins the host's key when it locks the host in: there must be exactly one host entry (an entry that claims to be a host without a valid signature still counts toward "two hosts"), and its `g` must check out against its own `k`; a host of another version has no key and is refused. The host pins a player's key when it gives the player a seat (the key in the player's own entry) and writes it in `mk`. After that a guest takes every other player's key from the host's signed seat list (never from the players' own entries), and a seat counts as ours only if `mk` holds this device's key. An entry that shows another key than the one pinned for its id never replaces the pin: that id is treated like two different entries under one id, believed for neither, until the other entry is gone (a friend then shows as having left, and a host that is no longer seen closes the lobby with the usual "host left" and, after the start, ends the shared base on the guests' devices after the usual 20 seconds). Nothing new is shown for it. The host's entry is used (for the start, the seat list, "the host is here") only while its `g` verifies.
- **Checking a message, in this order.** (1) a known event and an object with a `sig` of 86 base64url characters and an integer `q` from 1 to 2^40; (2) the size, at most the body limit plus `SIG_OVERHEAD`; (3) the sender is a seated player that is here, and may send this event (`st` and `rec` only the pinned host, `int` and `sync` only to the host, co-op events only in a co-op room); (4) a key is pinned for the sender; (5) the signature; (6) `q` is higher than the highest `q` already accepted from that sender (a copy of an older message is dropped; only a message that verified moves it); (7) the rate budget (status 8, final 3, react 3, pos 16, st 12, rec 24, int 24, sync 3 in 2 s per sender); (8) the parsers. The cheap checks come first, and the counter and the budget come after the signature, so a stranger who writes in a friend's name can use up neither the friend's counter nor the friend's budget.
- **The ordered pipeline.** Everything that arrives, presence lists and messages together, goes through one first-in, first-out queue (`Ordered`, 256 jobs at most); everything that leaves (messages and `track`) goes through another. With the real Web Crypto a check or a signature finishes later; the queue starts the next job only when the one before has finished, so a slow check can never let a later message overtake an earlier one, and a message that follows its sender's presence entry sees the pin that entry brought. The room checks that it is still the same room after every wait, so a room that was left drops what is in flight. With the synchronous test fake the queue runs inline, with no extra tick.
- **Injected crypto.** `CrewRoom` takes `options.crypto` (`generate()` and `verify()`, each may return a promise); the default is `webCrypto`. Tests give the room a deterministic synchronous fake (`tests/fake-crypto.ts`, set for every test file by `tests/setup-crypto.ts`) and the real thing in `tests/sign.test.ts`. The `?crew=local` transport and the Supabase transport are below the room, so both sign and check through the same code.
- **No downgrade.** There is no unsigned mode and no switch. A message without a valid signature, an entry without a key, a host without a valid signature and a peer of another version are all refused.

Room rules, in `src/net/room.ts`: the host made the room; a joiner who finds no host (or two) sees "no room"; four seats at most, given out by the host (a fifth sees "room full"; a freed seat goes to the next joiner); nobody joins after the start; if the host leaves before the start the room closes after 3.5 seconds; after the start nothing changes when someone leaves (they are marked as gone and their last numbers stay for the debrief); only the host's presence can start the mission (once, and it is a state, so a friend who was busy or lost the connection a moment still gets it); inbound messages from seated players are rate-limited per sender (status 8 per 2 s, stickers 3 per 2 s); coming back after a lost connection re-sends our numbers within the same sending limit. The co-op messages are passed through the room only when the start said `coop`: `st` and `rec` are accepted only from the locked host id and only by guests, `int` and `sync` only by the host, and a message that fails the checks is dropped whole (`src/net/coopwire.ts`). Inbound limits in 2 s per sender: `st` 12, `rec` 24, `int` 24, `sync` 3.

Code map:

- `src/net/protocol.ts` pure message types, validation, room codes, awards. No network, no screen.
- `src/net/sign.ts` signing and checking: the `CrewCrypto` interface, the Web Crypto implementation, the text that is signed, and the ordered queue.
- `src/net/follow.ts` the pure rules of the live avatars: smoothing, staleness, base slots, which friend is doing what, when a position is sent (`PositionSender`), who the next week waits for (`whoIsBehind`).
- `src/world/friends.ts` the friends' astronauts in the 3D world (uses `follow.ts`); `src/ui/CrewDock.tsx` also draws the name tags and sticker bubbles.
- `src/net/room.ts` the room as a state machine over a `Transport`.
- `src/net/transport.ts` the interface, `MemoryTransport` (tests), `LocalTransport` (two tabs, `?crew=local`).
- `src/net/supabase.ts` `SupabaseTransport`; the Supabase library itself is a separate chunk (about 23 kB), downloaded only when a room opens.
- `src/sim/coop.ts` the commander's rules of the shared base as plain data and pure functions (stages, ready sets, the vote, the walk merge, intents and how they are read, the countdowns on a clock that is passed in). No network, no screen, no timers.
- `src/net/coopwire.ts` the wire format of `st`, `rec`, `int` and `sync`: encoding, and reading back field by field with the checks listed above.
- `src/net/coop.ts` the session: `CoopHost` (runs the rules, sends `st` and `rec`, drops a seat that has been gone 5 s) and `CoopGuest` (mirrors `st`, files `rec`, queues and repeats intents, asks `sync`, detaches after 20 s without the commander). The numbers (heartbeat 4 s, retry 1.5 s, and so on) are in `COOP_NET`, the countdowns in `COOP_TIMING` (`src/sim/coop.ts`).
- `src/net/coopport.ts` turns the director's calls into intents; `src/net/coopglue.ts` joins a session to the director; `src/net/coopview.ts` the plain rules of the screens (who is waited for, who changed the plan, when "Go on" and "Play on alone" show).
- `src/game/director.ts` has the seam: with no port set (solo and race mode) every call runs the code it always did; with a port, a mutating call becomes an intent and the stage the commander is in is shown (`coopAdopt`).
- `src/net/crew.ts` signals for the screens and the glue to the game (`startMission`, the `mission` signal). Nothing runs until a player opens crew mode.
- `src/ui/CrewLobby.tsx`, `CrewDock.tsx`, `CrewResults.tsx`, `CrewStrip.tsx` (the dots, votes and countdowns of the shared base), `crew.css`, texts in `src/i18n/crew.ts`.

Crew missions use only the bundled real-history windows (`sunMode: 'featured'`), so every friend's game has exactly the same data even if one of them has fetched newer live data. The simulation never calls `Math.random`; `tests/crew.test.ts` plays whole missions twice with every test bot and checks they are identical.
`startMission` also saves the mission setup to the profile; crew mode puts the player's own setup back right after, so the next solo game is not changed.

## Try it on one computer

Open the game twice in the same browser with `?crew=local`, for example `http://localhost:5173/?crew=local`. The two tabs talk through `BroadcastChannel` (no internet needed). Make a room in one tab, join with the code in the other.

## Pointing it at another Supabase project

1. In the Supabase dashboard of your project, copy the project URL and the **publishable** (anon) key. Do not use a secret or service key; this key is shipped to every browser.
2. Broadcast and presence on public channels need no table and no policy. With the default Realtime settings of a new project they work as they do on the current project (checked with two clients: room, join, start, status, sticker).
3. Set the build variables (a `.env.local` file, or the Vercel project settings):

```
VITE_MP_URL=wss://YOUR-PROJECT-REF.supabase.co/realtime/v1
VITE_MP_KEY=sb_publishable_...
```

4. Allow the project in the Content Security Policy (`vercel.json`, `connect-src`), both the https and the wss host:

```
connect-src 'self' https://ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov https://YOUR-PROJECT-REF.supabase.co wss://YOUR-PROJECT-REF.supabase.co;
```

The defaults in `src/net/config.ts` point at the project `wvhdhfddtusppjmsgmvn`. Setting `VITE_MP_URL=` or `VITE_MP_KEY=` to an empty value hides crew mode (the "Play with friends" button disappears), except in the developer mode `?crew=local`, which needs no network.

## Tests

`npx vitest run tests/follow.test.ts` tests the live avatars: the smoothing, slots and waiting rules, the position sender (5 a second, only when changed, keepalive) and a position through rooms on the in-memory network. `npx vitest run tests/crew-wait.test.ts` tests the week wait from `src/net/crew.ts` with real rooms and a counter for the director: it goes on at once when nobody is behind, waits and then goes on once, "Don't wait", holding while a menu, film or building is open, release when a friend leaves or finishes, leaving the report, and the last week. `npx vitest run tests/crew.test.ts` runs the rooms on an in-memory network with fake timers: join, lobby, start with the same seed, status flow, stickers, throttles, malformed and oversized messages, a pretend host, a pretend start, the same presence key used twice, flood, room full, host leaves, lost connection, and determinism of the mission. The shared base has its own tests. `tests/coop.test.ts` (53) runs the commander's rules with two to four simulated friends through whole missions, with duplicate and stale intents, the vote and its ties, the countdowns, a leaver, the walk merge, and a balance check (a crew of four walkers must not beat the best solo walker). `tests/coopwire.test.ts` (39) checks that after every step of 72 whole missions what a guest rebuilds equals what the commander holds (minus the plan), the size caps, and that wrong, odd and hostile messages are refused. `tests/coopnet.test.ts` (32) runs the session over real rooms on the in-memory network with a clock that is passed in and a network that loses messages: the mirrors still agree. `tests/director-coop-host.test.ts` and `tests/director-coop-guest.test.ts` run the real director as commander and as guest (a guest in a room or a film when the stage changes, the commander's pause menu during the night, a commander who leaves and a guest who then plays on alone). `tests/coopview.test.ts`, `tests/crew-coop-friends.test.ts`, `tests/crew-start.test.ts` and `tests/crewresults.test.ts` cover the screen rules, the friends on a shared base, the start in both modes and the debrief. `tests/director.test.ts` (solo) is the same as on the deployed version.

Not covered by automated tests: the real Supabase connection (checked by hand with up to five real clients: room, join, fifth player refused, ready, start, status, sticker; the shared base has not yet been tried over real Supabase), the screens, including the 3D friends, name tags and the week wait (checked by hand in two browser tabs with `?crew=local`), and the shared base on real devices (a pass that is still to do: the kill switch stays closed until it is done).
